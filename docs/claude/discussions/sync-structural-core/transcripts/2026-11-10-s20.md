## The root cause

Cells without a `slide_id` get their identity from `(slide, kind, ordinal)`. Three consequences follow:

- **The ledger has no total order.** It records ordinals per slide and kind, plus order for id'd cells only (`doc_identity.py:377`). Cross-kind and cross-slide positions have to be reconstructed, which is what spans, crossed sync points, pool moves and placement rows do.
- **Cells are paired half-to-half before the ledger is consulted.** The lens pairs by cursor (`doc_lenses.py:590`), the differ re-pairs per pool (`sync_diff.py:3805`, with a second `legacy=True` algorithm at `:3381`), and the verify gate, `--stamp-ids`, `validate` and the executor's placement each pair independently. That is the divergence you noticed.
- **The plan is rows executed one at a time, then recorded by re-reading the files** (`doc_apply.py:2010`). Rows that are only valid together livelock, the engine reads its own write as an author edit, and each fix adds a deferral or freeze.

Since cutover the five core modules grew from 5,832 to 12,837 lines and the action vocabulary from 38 to 46. A subagent classified 40 sync issues from the issue and PR text (not from code):
- 20 of 36 with a deck shape involve an un-id'd cell, including all 11 wrong-pairing ones.
- Of 20 fixes, 9 changed a pairing algorithm and none was a generalization.
- At least 10 issues were filed as a consequence of an earlier fix.

## The replacement core

About 400 lines, no slide-scoped keys:

1. **Record order.** The ledger stores each half's full cell sequence.
2. **Align each half to the ledger, never half to half.** Match by id, then by exact content; what is left is read as "edited in place".
3. **Merge three ways.** Content and membership merge per cell. Order merges per pair of cells: if only one half changed whether x precedes y, take the change. A cycle is an order question.
4. **Ask one question where identity is unknown and matters:** "which of these cells is X?" It replaces the suspension and placement rows. Without it, the prototype reproduces your accepted residual (137 silent, 141 stops, all both-halves-move-and-edit).
5. **Render both halves from the one merged sequence,** so they agree by construction.

## Formalization

Feasible for this core, because its guarantees do not depend on the pairing heuristics. Lean is not installed here, so nothing was machine-proved. Instead I checked the laws exhaustively over every recorded deck up to 4 cells against every half up to 5:

- **Unchanged pair:** nothing happens (191 cases).
- **One half changed:** no question, and both halves equal the changed half (212,612 cases, 0 violations).
- **Swapping the halves:** same content in all 500,395 question-free merges; order differs in 128,848, which I attribute to the DE-first tie-break but did not classify.
- **Conservation:** no merge duplicates a cell or drops an addition. In 7,828 merges it keeps fewer copies than both halves hold; all have two byte-identical recorded cells where each half changed a different one. Your conservation gate withholds that shape; asking there is a policy choice.

A Lean proof of these laws looks like a few hundred lines of definitions and one to two thousand of proof. It would certify the model, not the Python, so I'd put the laws in CI first and treat Lean as optional.

## Limits

- The prototype is abstract: no text layer, tags, companions, id stamping or fork/unify. The probe doesn't exercise those either.
- Identity questions were answered by a perfect reader, like the probe's other policies.
- It stops at the first unanswered question; partial progress is designed but not prototyped.
- It needs a ledger schema change and a migration.
- The probe was built around the current engine's failures; a new core needs its own adversarial review.
- The issues with no un-id'd cell (answer contract, `vo_anchor`, docs) are untouched.
- The slide-hood issues (#1092, #1216, #1187) should dissolve because no key embeds the slide, but that is by construction, not measured.
